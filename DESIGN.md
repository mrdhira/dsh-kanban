# Design System: dsh-kanban Board

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "A departures board for work: the state of play legible in one glance, and every card's next legal move one interaction away."**

*This metaphor is **proposed by this regeneration and awaits Dhira's confirmation** — it has not been ratified. Everything else in this file is read from the code.*

The panel is a guest, not a host. It renders inside the DSH desktop app's left sidebar as the **Kanban** entry, under the heading **Board**, and it declares no palette, no font family and no page background of its own: every colour resolves through one of the app's `--dsw-*` theme tokens, so light and dark arrive for free and a theme change needs no edit here. PRODUCT.md makes this binding — "carrying its own palette is not on offer" — and the code obeys it down to the fallbacks, which exist only so a missing token degrades rather than breaks. Density is the aesthetic: 13px body text, a 7px working rhythm, five fixed 236px lanes that scroll sideways rather than compress, and a card whose whole summary is three short rows.

The surface is flat, hairline-driven and tonal. There is no `box-shadow` anywhere in the stylesheet, no gradient and no keyframe; depth is a 1px hairline, a radius that steps with the surface's importance, and the app's background tiers (a lane on `bg-layer-1`, a card on `bg-base`, a hover on `bg-layer-2`). What the panel *does* author is response: one 140ms ease-out transition on background, border and transform, a 1px press nudge, and a shared focus ring — all of it switched off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Colour is rationed to the point of austerity. The token named `brand-primary` resolves, in this app, to the same value as `label-primary` — so "the accent" is the foreground, not a hue, and the blue in the declared fallback never reaches the screen when the token is present. Red, amber and green each carry one meaning and no emphasis.

A distill pass reshaped the surface without removing a capability: the state-filter chip row is gone and creation is a title and `Add`; the metrics strip leads with two numbers and hides its history behind a disclosure; the drawer leads with one move and hides the rest behind `More moves…`; and the cards carry only what demands a decision. The disclosure is now a **first-class primitive** — a real `<button>` with `aria-expanded`, used twice on the panel (both in the metrics strip: `More metrics` and `Host`) and twice more inside the drawer (`More moves…`, `Exception to a gate…`) — and the two-number metrics strip is a **first-class component**, not a row of equal facts. Two later passes answered "the human lost single-state narrowing" and then un-answered it, both deliberately: the polish pass added one `select.kb-input` for it, and the third pass removed that select together with the whole `Board / Ageing / All / Dropped` view row on Dhira's verdict — *"Remove it, do not group by that, I think it's not common, just view all."* The board now draws **every state as a lane, `dropped` last**, so "view all" is literal, no control is needed to reach a dropped card, and single-state narrowing belongs to `kanban_list({ state: … })` and `bin/kanban.mjs --state` rather than to the panel. The panel's own toolbar is one search box, one title field and `Add`.

Typography is not a decision this panel makes: it inherits the app's face and authors only a scale (18/13/12px) plus one rule — monospace marks identity and measurement, not "technical" flavour. Card ids, lane counts, priority codes, flow metrics, history rows, the drawer's `rev · started · finished` line and the strip's `rev · host · source` line are monospace; titles, labels and buttons are not. Copy is plain and specific: controls name their action ("Dismiss", "Refresh", "Add", "Drop", "Unblock"), a refusal names the missing thing (`missing: outcome, acceptance`) instead of blaming the input, and every state that colour carries is also spelled out in words, because the light theme cannot carry amber as text.

Every value in this document is read from `src/client/styles.ts` and `src/client/components.ts`. The one externally-derived fact — what the app's tokens *resolve to* — was read again on 2026-10-16 from the installed app's own design-platform stylesheet inside `/Applications/DeepSeek Harness.app/Contents/Resources/app.asar`, by scanning the file in bounded chunks (the plain `body` block is light, the `body[data-ds-dark-theme]` block is dark); all fifteen tokens the sheet references resolved in both scopes, so no value here falls back to a literal. Nothing here is proposed except the North Star line above, and nothing is filled in where the code is silent — those places are listed under Open Questions.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Zero shadows, zero gradients, zero keyframes; depth comes from 1px hairlines, a radius step and the app's tint tiers.
- Fifteen `--dsw-*` tokens carry every colour (12 roles plus the scrollbar thumb/hover pair and the document-selection fill); `--dsw-alias-brand-primary` resolves to the same value as `--dsw-alias-label-primary`, so the accent is monochrome in practice.
- Three role fallback literals (`--kb-border-l1`, `--kb-bg-layer-2`, `--kb-label-secondary`) are declared once in `:root`, so a role has one alpha instead of the four `border-l1` had drifted into.
- One authored motion moment, stated as such in the sheet: 140ms `ease-out` on `background-color`, `border-color`, `transform` and the disclosure caret, with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` switching it off.
- Amber and `--dsw-alias-label-tertiary` are **dark-theme enhancements only**, because both fail AA on the light theme (2.15:1 and 3.71:1 on white).
- A disclosure primitive with `aria-expanded` — a real button, never `<details>` — carrying a drawn caret that flips, so its open state is not colour alone and not a pressed-button lookalike.
- The metrics strip leads with two numbers (`WIP n/limit`, `oldest Xd vs SLE Yd · N past`) and puts the cycle's median, 85th percentile and mean, throughput and the SLE's basis behind `More metrics`, and the board's revision and the transport's address behind `Host` beside it.
- Fixed 236px lanes in a horizontally scrolling row; an empty lane narrows to a content-width dashed slot (95px for `Review` at 13px, wider for a longer name) with its name and count on one horizontal line, and opens to full width while a drag is live.
- **No view row, and every state drawn as a lane.** The `Board / Ageing / All / Dropped` buttons and the `all states` select are removed; the board draws `backlog, ready, in-progress, review, done, dropped` in that order, so a dropped card is reachable without any control asking for it. The toolbar is the search box, the title field and `Add`.
- The drawer leads with one primary move; Block, Drop, their reasons, `Exception to a gate…` and the numeric position sit behind `More moves…`. A dropped card's primary move is the way back to the first lane.
- Monospace is reserved for identity, machine vocabulary and measurement.
- State is a 1px state-coloured border plus a word — never a thick side stripe, never colour alone.
- Browser surfaces are authored: `::selection`, `caret-color` and the board's scrollbar thumb come from the app's own tokens, not from the browser defaults.

## Colors

A borrowed, border-and-tint palette: greyscale structure inherited from the app, with exactly four coloured tokens — one accent and three meaning colours — carrying all of it. Every entry below records the token reference (which the frontmatter also carries, verbatim) and then the value the app's own stylesheet resolves it to in each theme; the reference is the source of truth, the resolution is context.

### Primary
- **Interactive accent — brand primary** (`var(--dsw-alias-brand-primary, #4c8bf5)` → `#0f1115` light / `#f9fafb` dark): the app maps this token onto its first foreground value, so on screen it is a near-black in light and a near-white in dark — the same value `label-primary` resolves to, **not** a hue. It marks a hovered card's border, every focus ring, and an informational notice's border. The `#4c8bf5` after the comma is a declared fallback the panel invented; with the token present it is never painted, and it is not one of the app's blue steps (`--dsw-static-blue-450` is `#4d93f8`).

### Secondary
- **Refusal red — state error** (`var(--dsw-alias-state-error-primary, #d64545)` → `#ec1313` light / `#f25a5a` dark): the "stopped" signal, in five places: the 1px border of a refusal or error notice, the 1px border of an `expedite` card, a lane count that has passed its WIP limit, the `P0` code in a card's top row, and a card whose age has passed `1.5 × SLE`. It measures 4.50:1 on the light surface and 5.55:1 on the dark one (4.77:1 on the dark card), so it is the one state colour allowed to be text in both themes.

### Tertiary
- **Attention amber — state warn** (`var(--dsw-alias-state-warn-primary, #d68945)` → `#f59e0b` in both themes): "needs attention, not stopped" — the 1px border of a blocked card in *both* themes, and, **in dark only**, the `P1` code, the `ageing` age and an unticked checklist row. It never carries **text** outside `body[data-ds-dark-theme]`, because on light it measures 2.15:1 on white; as a 1px border it is not text, and it is not gated.
- **Completion green — state success** (`var(--dsw-alias-state-success-primary, var(--dsw-alias-label-primary, inherit))` → `#22c55e` in both themes): one consumer, the drawn frame of the tick that marks a `done` card's top row. Its fallback chain ends at the ordinary label colour, so a missing token degrades to "marked" rather than to nothing. **The frame carries the hue; the tick inside it is drawn in `label-primary`**, because `#22c55e` composites to only 2.28:1 on the light card's white and no stroke width can take a colour past its own ratio — the tick is the part that must clear 3:1, and it clears it at 18.90:1 (measured below).

### Neutral
- **Foreground — label primary** (`var(--dsw-alias-label-primary, inherit)` → `#0f1115` light / `#f9fafb` dark): the page's text colour, set once on `.kb-page` and inherited by everything; controls and inputs re-state `color: inherit` rather than naming the token again.
- **De-emphasis — label secondary** (`var(--dsw-alias-label-secondary, rgba(128,128,128,0.9))` → `#61666b` light / `#cfd3d6` dark): the panel's de-emphasis device. It carries the sub-lines, the lane count, card ids, the card meta row, the notice's `missing:` list, `.kb-dim` rows, empty-lane text, every placeholder, the **disabled** control's label, the whole board row while a read or a write is in flight, and — as `currentColor` — the dashed drop ring. It is 5.80:1 light / 12.11:1 dark on a card and 5.80:1 / 10.42:1 on a lane, so it is the one token that can de-emphasise text and still pass AA.
- **Dimmest tier — label tertiary** (`var(--dsw-alias-label-tertiary, var(--dsw-alias-label-secondary, rgba(128,128,128,0.9)))` → `#81858c` light / `#adb2b8` dark): a dark-theme-only *enhancement* over `label-secondary`, applied to the header sub-lines, placeholders and empty-lane text. It is 8.54:1 in dark (7.36:1 on a lane's own tier) but only 3.71:1 on light, so it is named only inside `body[data-ds-dark-theme]`.
- **Hairline — border l1** (`var(--dsw-alias-border-l1, rgba(128,128,128,0.35))` → `#0000000a` light / `#ffffff0f` dark): every resting border in the panel — buttons, disclosure toggles, chips, inputs, notices, lanes, cards, the drawer, and the two dividers that open a `More moves…` fold and set off the destructive group. It is the system's primary structural device.
- **Stronger hairline — border l2** (`var(--dsw-alias-border-l2, var(--dsw-alias-border-l1, rgba(128,128,128,0.5)))` → `#0000001a` light / `#ffffff1f` dark): a pressed button's border and an input's hover border. Two consumers, both state.
- **Card surface — bg base** (`var(--dsw-alias-bg-base, transparent)` → `#fff` light / `#151517` dark): a card's background. In light it is the same `#fff` the lane carries; the card is separated from the lane by its hairline and radius, not by tone.
- **Lane surface — bg layer 1** (`var(--dsw-alias-bg-layer-1, rgba(128,128,128,0.04))` → `#fff` light / `#232324` dark): the lane's and the parked slot's background — the only large tinted area on the board, and the reason a card reads as raised without a shadow. In light the lane is white on the sidebar's own `#f9fafb`, and `bg-layer-1`, `bg-layer-2` and `bg-base` all resolve to that same white, so no tint distinguishes the card there (see the slot paragraph below).
- **Raised tint — bg layer 2** (`var(--dsw-alias-bg-layer-2, rgba(128,128,128,0.12))` → `#fff` light / `#2c2c2e` dark): button hover, a pressed button's fill, a lane a card is over, and the `.kb-chip-kb` chip that marks the active sprint.
- The page itself paints no background: `.kb-page`, `.kb-notice`, `.kb-drawer`, `.kb-btn` and `.kb-input` are all transparent, so the host's panel fill shows through.

### Measured contrast

Every ratio below is computed from the app's resolved token values (read 2026-10-16 from the installed `app.asar`, the surface named in each row — not the doc's convenience surface). The reproduction is `test/client.dom.test.ts`'s `RESOLVED` table plus its own WCAG luminance helper, so these numbers come from the same table the suite uses. **Only seven cells are actually asserted** (the opacity, group and scrollbar rows); the rest were computed once and are not re-verified — read an unasserted row as a record of a measurement, not as a guarantee. One wrong number survived here for exactly that reason, and a reviewer found it, not the suite. AA text is **4.5:1**; the graphical floor, and the floor for a control's own boundary, is **3:1**.

### Named Rules
**The Borrowed Palette Rule.** The panel owns no colour. Every value resolves to a `--dsw-*` token; the literal after the comma is a fallback for a missing token, never a colour to design with. A new screen that needs a colour needs a token the app already provides.

**The Accent-Is-The-Foreground Rule.** `brand-primary` and `label-primary` resolve to the same value in this app. Nothing may assume the accent is a hue: a design that leans on "blue" leans on a fallback that is not painted, and a state that must be seen needs weight, a border or a word, not the brand token.

**The AA-Per-Theme Rule.** A colour that fails AA on light may carry text only inside `body[data-ds-dark-theme]`, where it is a redundant enhancement over a default that passes. Amber (`#f59e0b`, 2.15:1 on white) and `label-tertiary` (`#81858c`, 3.71:1) are the two, and the panel's own test refuses any unscoped text use of either.

**The Meaning-Only Rule.** Red means refused, excepted, P0 or stale; amber means blocked, P1, ageing or unticked; green means done. None is available for emphasis, decoration or emphasis-by-size.

## Typography

**Display Font:** inherited from the host app — the panel declares no `font-family` for prose
**Body Font:** inherited from the host app (13px/1.5 base set on `.kb-page`, as `font-size` and `line-height`; **never** as the `font` shorthand)
**Label/Mono Font:** `ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, Menlo, monospace` (declared inline, seven times)

**Character:** deliberately faceless. The panel takes the app's letterforms so it reads as part of the same product, and spends its only typographic voice on a single distinction: proportional for prose, monospace for anything that is an identifier, a count, a machine value or a measurement.

The base type is written as `font-size: 13px; line-height: 1.5` and not as `font: 13px/1.5 inherit`. The shorthand was the panel's first and worst defect: a family component in the `font` shorthand must be a family (or a system keyword), and `inherit` is neither, so the browser dropped the **whole declaration** and the panel painted at the app body's 16px — measured across all 84 probe states as `font-size: 16px` against `bodySize: 16px`, roughly 23% larger than every number in this document. The family remains unauthored, because inheriting the app's face is the documented position; only the size and the leading are declared, and a test walks the sheet for the shorthand that carries `inherit` as a family.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (600, 18px, 1.25): the board heading — the store's board title, defaulting to "Kanban" while the first payload is in flight. One per panel, `margin: 0`.
- **Body** (400, 13px, 1.5, inherited family): everything else — card titles, button labels, field values, checklist labels, notice text, drawer rows (which step down to 12px in `.kb-list`). Line length is governed by the panel's narrow column, except where `.kb-prose` caps a long acceptance line at `62ch` with `text-wrap: pretty`.
- **Label** (400, 12px): monospace where it marks data — card ids, lane counts, priority codes, flow metrics, the sprint chip, history rows, the `missing:` list, and the header's count sub-line plus the strip's `rev · host · source` line — and the same 12px in the inherited face for chips and the card meta row. The metrics strip additionally sets `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`, so a changing number does not shift its neighbours.

### Named Rules
**The Monospace Is Data Rule.** Monospace marks an identifier or a measurement — card id, lane count, priority code, metrics, history, the header's count line, the `missing:` vocabulary — never "technical" flavour, never a title, never body copy.

**The One Heading Rule.** The panel has exactly one heading, at 18px/600. Section labels inside the drawer are 12px dim spans (`detail`, `move`, `block`, `drop`, `Definition of Ready`, `history`), not headings — the scale has no second display step.

## Layout

A single flex column filling the panel: `.kb-page` is `display: flex; flex-direction: column; height: 100%; min-height: 0`, with a 10px gap between the header, the toolbar, any notices, the metrics strip, the lanes, the sprint bar and the drawer, and `14px 16px 18px` of padding.

**The board is the horizontal axis.** `.kb-lanes` is a `display: flex` row with `overflow-x: auto`, `align-items: flex-start`, `flex: 1 1 auto`, `gap: 10px`, `padding-bottom: 6px` and a `min-height: 140px` floor that keeps the drawer from eating the board. Each `.kb-lane` is `flex: 0 0 236px` **with `min-width: 0`** — it can neither grow nor shrink — so the board always scrolls sideways instead of reflowing; only the empty lane opts out, to `flex: 0 0 auto` at its content width (see the slot paragraph below). At the ~1000px sidebar column PRODUCT.md records, six 236px lanes plus five 10px gaps plus 32px of page padding cannot fit; horizontal scrolling is the normal state, not a fallback. (That arithmetic is derived from the observed values and the recorded column width; it is not itself a token.)

**`min-width: 0` is what makes `flex: 0 0 236px` true, and it is not decoration.** A flex base size is not a cap: a flex item's **automatic minimum size** (`min-width: auto`, i.e. its min-content width) still wins, so one unbreakable token in one card title set one lane's min-content — and therefore its rendered width — to **603.03px against its own declared 236px**, in every state, theme and width the probe measures (66 findings). The token that did it is ordinary: the probe's fixture carries a 64-character payload digest and a 23-character session id, the shape a real board has in a bug title. `min-width: 0` lets the declared basis hold, and `.kb-card-title`'s `overflow-wrap: break-word` is the other half — the token breaks inside the card's 196px content box instead of pushing the card, and through it the lane, past the sheet's own number.

**Six lanes, every state — and no view controls at all.** `BOARD_STATES` is `STATES` itself, in store order, so `columnsOf` draws Backlog, Ready, In Progress, Review, Done and **Dropped last**. There is no view row: the `Board / Ageing / All / Dropped` buttons and the `all states` select are removed, and the filter that remains is a *card* filter, not a lane filter — no value of `ViewFilter` can take a lane away. The `dropped` lane at the end is what keeps a dropped card reachable from the panel (`board.db`, the saved `views`, the host API and `bin/kanban.mjs --state` all still carry that capability for the tools; the panel simply no longer offers a switcher). The toolbar is the search input, the new-card title and `Add`. The header's sub-line states the count plainly — `M cards` when every card the board holds is drawn, `showing X of Y cards` whenever what is drawn is fewer, from the search or from a truncated read — because `rev N` is developer vocabulary: the revision lives behind `Host` in the metrics strip. The chip row that used to repeat the lane headers here is gone, and nothing replaced it.

**Pending is a state the board row carries.** While the first read is in flight the board still draws its six lanes — from `BOARD_STATES`, the board's own state order, not from any saved view or default DoW — with no card, no invented count and **no cap**: the Definition of Workflow arrives with the payload, so a pending lane prints its name and a bare `0` rather than a `0/N` that could contradict the board it is standing in for. Each lane holds one dashed `.kb-skeleton` line where a card will land, so a pending board reads as pending instead of as an empty one. The row carries `aria-busy` and de-emphasises its text with the `label-secondary` token (`kb-lanes-loading`); a write in flight uses the same row and a different class (`kb-lanes-busy`). **The de-emphasis is a colour, never an element opacity**: dimming the whole row took the ids, the lane counts, the card titles and the comments to 2.53:1 light / 3.59:1 dark — a board that fails AA for exactly as long as it is being written to. As a colour, the parts that set their own tone (an over-limit count, a `P0` code, the drawn mark) keep it, so a warning survives the write. Nothing here is animated and nothing spins: the words in the header (`connecting…`) and the state on the row are the whole treatment. A board that *answered* with nothing is a different thing: its lanes keep the `.kb-empty` **rule** the sheet has always had — the markup is the `.kb-cards` branch's `nothing here` span — but every lane with no cards is a slot, and a slot hides that span at rest (`.kb-lane-empty` sets `display: none`; the drag-live `.kb-lane-awake` restores it). The line is styled and unrendered at rest, deliberately; see the slot paragraph below and open question 20. The one defect left in this area — a *forced* read landing mid-write can still replace the optimistic payload, though the timer's own poll can no longer clear the busy flag or the card — is recorded under *Known defects*.

**No filter changes which lanes are drawn, and a dropped card is the one card whose primary move is a reopen.** `filterCards` narrows *cards* — the panel's only filter is the search box, and the saved views the payload still carries are deliberately not offered as controls — so `columnsOf` returns the same six columns for every filter and a `state: ['dropped']` filter removes the cards in the other five lanes without removing the lanes. A dropped card's lane is last because `dropped` is the end of the store's state order, and its drawer leads with `→ Backlog`: `dropped` is not a step in the workflow, it is where a card goes when it leaves, so "the next lane" has no answer for it and the way back in is the first lane. Nothing else about a dropped card is special: it keeps the card shape and hairline, takes the dim tier in `.kb-card-dropped`, and moves through the same store call as any other card.

**Dropped is the one lane that refuses the gesture, and the gesture opens the drawer instead.** A move to `dropped` needs a written reason — `checkMoveLocally` in the client half and `policy.checkMove` on the host both require one — and a drag carries no reason, so a drop on that lane can never complete. The lane therefore does **not** paint the legal-target treatment: `isDropAllowed` returns false for `(from, dropped)` whenever the card is not already there, which is the same predicate the drop itself reads, so the affordance and the behaviour cannot drift. The gesture is not discarded: `stagesInsteadOfMoving` routes it to the drawer with the drop staged, the `Drop` group revealed (it lives behind `More moves…`, and a staged move must show the field it is asking for), the reason field focused, and the drop group tinted with `bg-layer-2` — a tonal step, never a colour, and the staged line inside it is what says why. **The reason field takes the keyboard on every staged path**: a drag, the keyboard's `Alt+→`, and the drawer's own `Move … to Dropped` button, which is a click and used to pass `'keyboard'` regardless of device, so it staged the group and left the field unfocused while the store's own doc said otherwise. The effect now keys on the staged fact and the field's presence, not on the gesture's label. The `Drop` group is a **named `role="group"`** (`Dropped, needs a written reason to confirm`) — the one box on the panel that has to be read as a unit, and a name on a role-less `div` is inert. Nothing is sent until the human confirms. **The only way a card reaches `dropped` is a confirmed drawer move carrying a reason**, and that holds for every path: the pointer's drag, the keyboard's `Alt+→`, the drawer's own `Move … to Dropped` button, and any later control that targets the state all pass through the same route. The confirm itself rides `store.drop`, which is the one call that puts the reason on the wire; the drop index the pointer chose travels with the staged move, so a confirmed drop lands in the gap the human aimed at rather than at the end of the lane. Two smaller consequences are deliberate: a card dropped on the lane it already occupies is a no-op rather than a same-state move that writes only `expectedRev`, and a card already in `dropped` takes no staging (there is nothing left to ask for). The real-browser proof lives in `scripts/drag-probe.mts` — on demand, not in the fast suite — because hit-testing and the browser's own drag pipeline are exactly what jsdom cannot show.

**The probe waits on commits, and one product effect exists to publish them.** That probe was flaky the day it landed — 3 of 6 runs failed, in two different observations, always with the board unmoved: the wait, not the panel. Two of its reads raced a commit. "A card is in the dropped lane" is true of the *previous* render tree too, so a coordinate could be measured against a node the next commit removed, and the drag events then arrived at no lane at all; and the drawer's `data-staged` and its focused reason field arrive one commit *after* the drawer element, because `stageDrop` records the stage and then awaits the drawer's read while the Drop group is revealed by an effect. Reading the drawer as soon as it existed reported a panel that had staged correctly as one that had not. Both waits are now on facts a commit produces: `BoardPage` publishes `renderedRev` and a `stageable` mark through an optional `__mark` on the document's own window — inert unless a page provides one, which only the probe does — and the probe waits for that mount's board, not for any board. Nothing about the panel's behaviour changed; the store-level contract the probe depends on (a staged drop survives a poll, and is recorded in the same write as the selection) is pinned by `D8` in `test/client.dom.test.ts`.

**An empty lane is a slot, not a void — and it says so without the empty-state line.** A lane with no cards takes `.kb-lane-empty`: `flex: 0 0 auto`, `align-items: stretch`, a `border-style: dashed` edge, and a header set `flex-direction: row` so the name and the count sit on **one horizontal line**; its card list keeps `width: 100%` with `min-height: 48px`, and its `.kb-empty` span is `display: none`. (It restates no padding: the 9px is `.kb-lane`'s, and the slot is a delta on it.) Four empty lanes at 236px each would scroll for nothing, and the slot is content-width — 95.44px for `Review` at 13px, wider for a longer name — which is what distinguishes it from its siblings. The dashed edge is decorative, not the signal: it is the lane's own `border-l1` (`#0000000a`, compositing to `#f5f5f5` — 1.09:1 against the white lane in light, 1.19:1 in dark), and no fill carries meaning either, because in light `bg-layer-1`, `bg-layer-2` and `bg-base` all resolve to the same colour. **The slot is the only place the line is hidden**, and the hide is deliberate rather than incidental: `nothing here` is the text that would make it 236px. What carries the state instead is the slot's own contents, stated twice over — the lane name (horizontally, at 600) and the count (`0`, or `0/N` when the state has a cap) — with the same facts in the lane's `aria-label` (`"Review, 0 cards, limit 1"`, plus `", over the limit"` when past the cap). No rule in the sheet turns text on its side: the sheet now declares **no `writing-mode` at all**, and a test asserts that over every spelling, `sideways-lr` included. The slot and the awake lane are one orientation — `flex-direction: row` on the header in both — so starting or ending a drag cannot flip a header from a column to a row. The moment a drag begins, `.kb-lane-awake` restores the full 236px **and the empty-state text** (`display: block`): a drop target that cannot be hit is not a drop target, and a full-width lane has room for the words. A first load is never confused with an empty answer, because a pending lane draws a `.kb-skeleton` line in place of the `.kb-empty` branch, and the row is `aria-busy`. A lane also has a `min-height: 110px` of its own.

**Vertical structure inside a lane:** a header row (name at 600, count in monospace, and an "over limit" pill when the state has a cap), then the card list at `min-height: 36px`. Lane count text is `M` for uncapped states and `M/N` for Ready (`readyCap`), In Progress (WIP) and Review (WIP); a count past its limit turns red. `.kb-empty` is `color: label-secondary; padding: 10px 2px` — a styled line, kept in the sheet for the state it was written for (the awake lane) even though the parked slot hides it at rest; it is the `.kb-cards` empty branch's markup, not a rule the board draws.

**Narrowness is absorbed by wrapping, not by breakpoints.** There is exactly one media query in the stylesheet and it is motion, not width: `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)`. There are no `@container` or `@supports` rules. The five containers that wrap are the header (`.kb-head`), every control row (`.kb-bar`, which covers the toolbar, the drawer's groups and the comment row), the card's chip row (`.kb-card-meta`) and the drawer header (`.kb-drawer-head`). Two rows set the opposite: the card's top row is `flex-wrap: nowrap` with `white-space: nowrap` on its children, because four items in a 236px lane must never split a `T-005` mid-token, and the metrics strip is a wrapping row whose own items never break. The notice and the `More moves…` fold are fixed columns; everything else is fixed or scrolls.

**The drawer** is the last child, inline below the board: `max-height: 260px` with its own `overflow: auto`. The cap is in pixels, not a share of the page — the previous `max-height: 46%` let a single-card board take the lanes' half of the panel to open one card. The lanes keep their own 140px floor above it. Its children are stacked at a 9px gap, and its inner 12px lists sit at a 3px gap.

**Spacing rhythm.** Spacing is literal pixels, not variables — with **one** exception, the control height, which is a `--kb-*` property because three rules share it (`.kb-btn`, `.kb-input` and, through the button vocabulary, `.kb-more`) and a height that drifted between them was this pass's measured defect. It clusters: **3px** for list and checklist gaps and the control-group gap; **5–6px** inside a card (5px between rows, 6px in the meta row); **7px** as the working rhythm (card gaps, lane gaps, card padding, card top-row gap, the `More moves…` fold's and the danger group's top padding, and — as a radius — buttons, inputs, notices); **8px** for the toolbar gap, the input/check gaps **and the left inset of a control in a bar** — the button, the input and the disclosure all pad 8px, where they used to be 9/8/7 and read as three edges; **9px** for lane padding, drawer row gaps and the drawer's group gap; **10px** for page section gaps, panel radii, the notice gap and the drawer-header gap; **11–14px** for notice padding, drawer padding and the metrics gap; **14/16/18px** for page padding; **26px** for the control height.

## Elevation & Depth

**This system has no shadows.** `box-shadow` appears zero times in the stylesheet, and so do gradients and keyframes. Depth is tonal and linear: a 1px hairline on every surface, a radius that steps with the surface's importance (7px control → 8px card → 10px lane and drawer), and the app's tint tiers — the lane carries `bg-layer-1`, the card carries `bg-base`, a hovered or pressed control carries `bg-layer-2`. The panel paints no page background at all, so the app's own sidebar fill is the ground.

Two outline treatments stand in for elevation-as-feedback, and both are reserved: a lane that a card is over **and that could complete the drop** gets a 2px **dashed** `label-secondary` outline inset 3px on a `bg-layer-2` fill (unmistakably a drop affordance, not a raised surface), and focus gets a 2px **solid** brand outline — 2px offset on buttons, inputs, checklist rows and the drawer, 1px on a card, which sits inside the horizontally scrolled lane. The dashed treatment is exactly as wide as the drops that can be sent: `Dropped` never receives it, because a drag cannot carry the reason its gate requires and the gesture hands over to the drawer instead (the Refuse-and-Hand-Over Rule).

**Motion is one authored moment, not a scattering of effects.** Buttons transition `background-color`, `border-color` and `transform` over 140ms `ease-out`; inputs transition `background-color` and `border-color`; a button press nudges `translateY(1px)`. There is no entrance animation, no keyframe and no looping effect, and `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` sets `transition: none` on both and cancels the press nudge. PRODUCT.md's reduced-motion commitment is honoured by construction: no transition is load-bearing.

A disclosure's reveal is therefore instantaneous, not animated: opening `More metrics`, `Host`, `More moves…` or `Exception to a gate…` shows the revealed half in the same frame, separated from the row it opens from by a `border-l1` top hairline and 7px of padding rather than by motion.

The browser surfaces are **authored**, not inherited: `::selection` takes the app's `--dsw-alias-bg-document-selection`, the caret takes `--dsw-alias-label-primary`, and the board's sideways scroll and the drawer's body scrollbar take the app's own scrollbar tokens — `--dsw-alias-scrollbar-bg-l1` for the thumb and `--dsw-alias-scrollbar-hover-l1` for its hover, over a **transparent track**, written for both the standard `scrollbar-color`/`scrollbar-width` pair and the `::-webkit-scrollbar-*` set. Each keeps a literal fallback like every other value here. **The transparent track is the app's own answer to a duplicated token, not a preference**: `scrollbar-bg-l1` and `-bg-l2` both resolve to `--dsw-static-neutral-200` in light, so a filled track would paint the thumb at 1.00:1 and the scrollbar would read as a gap; the app's own `scrollbar-color` is `var(--dsh-scrollbar-thumb) transparent` and its `::-webkit-scrollbar-track` is `background: 0 0`. This panel writes the same thing, which is why `-bg-l2` is the one name in the ladder the sheet does not reference — and its fallback story ("`transparent`") was a claim the doc made about a value the token never held. The craft floor calls this the cheapest signal that a page was built rather than assembled, and it was the one surface where the panel had been taking the host's defaults.

### Named Rules
**The No-Shadow Rule.** Surfaces are flat at rest and flat under the pointer. Depth comes from a hairline, a radius and a tint step, never from `box-shadow`; a raised look is bought with `bg-layer-2`, not blur.

**The Refuse-and-Hand-Over Rule.** A gesture that cannot finish a move does not pretend otherwise. Where a gate needs a written reason the drag cannot supply — today only `dropped` — the lane withholds the legal-target treatment and the gesture hands the move to the drawer, which is the one place that can complete it: same predicate for the highlight and the drop, nothing sent until the human confirms. An affordance that accepts a gesture and then refuses it after the card has snapped back is the defect this rule exists to prevent.

**The Response-Not-Entrance Rule.** Motion exists only as a 140ms ease-out answer to state, and every transition is switched off wholesale under `prefers-reduced-motion`. No element animates into existence — including a disclosure's revealed half.

## Shapes

The form language is **rectilinear and lightly rounded** — one radius scale applied by surface role, and nothing else. Controls (buttons, inputs, disclosure toggles, notices) take 7px; cards take 8px; lanes, the parked slot and the drawer take 10px; chips are fully pill-shaped at 999px. The sidebar icon rounds its three bars with `rx: 1.2` in a 20-unit viewBox, and the two drawn check glyphs use `rx: 2.5` in a 16-unit viewBox — the same family at icon scale.

Every shape is closed by a 1px hairline in `border-l1`. There is no border-radius mixing, no clipping (`overflow` is only ever `auto`, for the lane row and the drawer body), no irregular silhouette and no geometric mask. A disclosure's revealed half is not a box of its own: `.kb-fold` shares the drawer's surface and is set off from the row above it by a full-width 1px hairline — the same device that separates the destructive group from the reversible moves.

**The state edge is 1px, all the way round.** A card that needs to shout is marked by recolouring its whole 1px border — red for `expedite`, amber for blocked — never by a thick one-sided stripe. The state itself is already named in the card's own content (the `expedite` chip, the `blocked` chip, the priority code), so the border is a redundant edge, not the message. Notices follow the same rule: a refusal or error notice recolours its 1px border red, an informational one recolours it to the brand token.

The card's own geometry is a three-row stack: an identity row (an optional drawn completion mark, then id, priority code, age) on a shared baseline that must never wrap, a title row that takes the remaining width, and a wrapping meta row of chips. Nothing is clipped; a long title wraps rather than truncating.

## Components

The vocabulary is one stylesheet of `kb-`-prefixed classes and plain `React.createElement` calls. Every `kb-` class used by `components.ts` is authored by a rule in `styles.ts`, including every member of the three template-generated families (`kb-age-{fresh|ageing|stale}`, `kb-pri-{p0..p4}`, `kb-notice-{refusal|info|error}`) — the members that carry no colour state the resting tier instead of inheriting it. `kb-card-done`, which an earlier revision pushed onto a done card without a rule, is gone from both halves; so is the bare `kb-age` hook, which the walk below found emitted and unstyled. **A test walks every class the components file emits** — the string tokens and template prefixes in its source, expanded over the three families — **against the sheet**, so a hook that styles nothing cannot come back: `P4.11` fails on a re-added `kb-lane-loading` or `kb-age`.

### Buttons
- **Shape:** 7px radius, 1px `border-l1` border, transparent background, `padding: 3px 8px`, **`height: 26px`** (`--kb-control-height`, with `box-sizing: border-box`), `font: inherit`, `line-height: 1.4`, `cursor: pointer`. The height is the whole control-height scale: measured, the button's natural box was 30.39px (an inline-flex disclosure's 9px caret sets a taller line box than the text does) while an input beside it measured 26.00px, so the panel's rows held two heights. The old `min-height: 24px` was a *floor*, never a cap, and the 30.39px box passed it. One height now covers the button, the input and the select, and the 8px inset is shared with both of the others, so one bar has one height and one left edge.
- **Primary:** there is no button hierarchy. One `.kb-btn` serves header actions, view controls, drawer moves, Block/Unblock, Drop, Comment, Dismiss, Close, the exception and set-position actions, and the checklist rows — the same quiet outline in every case, distinguished only by its label.
- **Hover:** `background: bg-layer-2` and nothing else — no border, colour, shadow or transform change. **Active:** `translateY(1px)`.
- **Pressed (`[aria-pressed="true"]`):** the fill becomes `bg-layer-2`, the border steps up to `border-l2`, and the weight goes to `font-weight: 600`. It is deliberately never a colour on its own: `brand-primary` resolves to near-white in dark, so a brand *background* painted body-white while the unselected button read stronger — the tint-plus-weight cue survives both themes and a greyscale screenshot. Its remaining consumer is the ticked checklist row; the active saved view and the unfiltered `All` toggle went with the view row.
- **Disabled:** `color: label-secondary; cursor: default`, with hover, the press nudge **and any pressed fill** cancelled, so a control that is both chosen and disabled (a ticked checklist row a mutation has taken out of action) reads as inert rather than as chosen. **It is not dimmed with an element opacity**: `opacity: 0.5` on the whole button took the label to 3.51:1 in light and its 1px hairline with it, so the label token is what does the de-emphasising now (5.80:1 light / 12.11:1 dark on the card). Used by `Add` with an empty title, `Drop`/`Block` with an empty reason, `Comment` with an empty body, `Move with exception` without a reason, `Set position` before the number changes, and every checklist row while the store's `busy` is set — a tick that cannot land is not offered. The button that would create nothing is present but inert.
- **Focus:** `:focus-visible` draws a 2px solid brand outline at 2px offset, authored for buttons, inputs, checklist rows and the drawer in one selector list; a card gets the same ring at 1px offset.

### Disclosure / Toggle
- **Shape:** the button vocabulary plus `.kb-more` — `display: inline-flex`, `padding: 1px 8px`, 12px type, inside the shared 26px control height. It is the smallest control on the panel by *type* — it names what it reveals and competes with nothing — but not by target or by height: a bar holding one is not a row of two heights, and its 8px inset matches the button's and the input's.
- **State:** `aria-expanded` is the whole state, and the toggle is a real `<button type="button">` with a stable label — a control that renames itself is two controls to a reader. An open disclosure takes the same treatment as a pressed button (tint, `border-l2`, weight 600) and is told apart from one by its caret: a drawn 9px chevron pointing right when closed, rotated a quarter-turn when open, `aria-hidden` so the accessible name stays exactly the label.
- **Reveal:** the revealed half is a sibling rendered inline in the row it belongs to, never a block the browser styles: a `<span>` beside the metrics, the `rev · host · source` span inside the metrics strip under `Host`, a `.kb-fold` under `More moves…`. Not `<details>`, deliberately — the opened half must render as part of its row.
- **The two weights:** only `aria-expanded` gets the weight bump. `Exception to a gate…` and `More moves…` additionally carry `aria-expanded` while sitting in `.kb-bar`; a nested disclosure (`Exception to a gate…` inside `More moves…`'s fold) is allowed, and the fold is separated by a `border-l1` hairline rather than by indentation.
- **Consumers:** `More metrics` and `Host` (both in the metrics strip), `More moves…` (the drawer's move group), `Exception to a gate…` (inside the fold). `More metrics`, `Host`, `More moves…` and `Exception to a gate…` also carry a `data-action` for the DOM suite; the label stays fixed in every case.

### Chips
- **Style:** pill (999px), 1px `border-l1` border, transparent background, `padding: 1px 8px`, 12px.
- **State:** a chip is never selectable, never a filter, and carries no pressed or active variant — but it is not always neutral. Three variants exist: `.kb-chip-kb` adds `bg-layer-2` and marks the active sprint; `.kb-chip-warn` colours the `over limit` chip in `state-error` at 600 weight, so the lane's warning is a colour **and** a word rather than a coloured count beside an ordinary chip; every other chip is informational. On a card they show only the four facts that demand a decision: `blocked`, the P0/P1 word, an age band that has left `fresh`, and `expedite`. Class, assignee, project and sprint are facts about the card rather than about what to do next, and they live in the drawer. In the drawer header the chips state, priority, class and age.

### Cards / Containers
- **Corner Style:** 8px.
- **Background:** `bg-base` on the lane's `bg-layer-1`. In light both resolve to `#fff`, so the card reads through its hairline and radius; in dark the card is a step lighter than the lane.
- **Shadow Strategy:** none — see Elevation & Depth.
- **Border:** 1px `border-l1`, recoloured whole to red for `expedite` and amber for blocked. A card can carry both classes; the blocked rule is declared second, so on a card that is both, amber wins.
- **Priority code:** `P0`–`P4` in 600-weight monospace, built by slicing the priority label. **Only `P0` and `P1` carry a colour**, and `P1`'s is dark-only: `P0` is red in both themes, `P1` is the foreground in light and amber under `body[data-ds-dark-theme]`. `P2`–`P4` are explicitly `label-primary` rather than left to inheritance, so every member of the family is authored. The word in the chip row (`high`) is not decoration — it is how the priority survives the light theme.
- **Age:** `3d` / `0.5d` / `—`, banded against the SLE. `stale` (past `1.5 ×` SLE) is red **and** bold in both themes; `ageing` (past the SLE) is the foreground in light and amber in dark; `fresh` is explicitly `label-secondary`, so the three bands are authored rather than two authored and one inherited. The band is only printed as a chip once it has left `fresh`.
- **Completion mark:** a `done` card carries a drawn 14px glyph at the head of its top row — a rounded frame in `state-success` with the tick inside it drawn in `label-primary`. The frame carries the meaning (redundant with the lane and the word `Done`); the tick carries the contrast, because the success hue composites to only 2.28:1 on the card's light surface and a stroke cannot exceed its own colour's ratio. It is a mark, not an edge — a done card and a review card are otherwise the same shape and hairline — and it is `aria-hidden`, because the card's own label already names the state.
- **Internal Padding:** `7px 9px`, with a 5px gap between the three rows, 7px in the top row and 6px in the meta row. A card with nothing to flag is exactly three rows: no empty chip row is rendered.
- **States:** hover promotes the **border** to the brand token; `:focus-visible` draws a 2px brand outline at offset 1px; `cursor: grab` at rest. No dragging cursor is declared. A card is `draggable`, `tabIndex: 0`, `role="button"`, labelled `"{id} {title}, {state}, {priority}"`, carries `aria-keyshortcuts="Alt+ArrowLeft Alt+ArrowRight"` and `data-card="{id}"`, and opens on **`Enter` or `Space`** — the APG button pattern, both keys, because a `role="button"` div gets neither for free and a keyboard user who pressed Space was scrolling the page instead. The shortcut is declared on the card only. The lane used to carry the same `aria-keyshortcuts` on a div that is never focusable, where `aria-keyshortcuts` means "the shortcut this element offers" and no reader can act on it; that declaration is gone, and the lane now has the role it always was — `role="group"` with its `aria-label`, which names the lane, its card count, its limit whenever the state has one, and `over the limit` whenever the count has passed it, so the name says what the header's `1/5` and its chip say. A card in `dropped` state — drawn in the last lane, no longer behind a view — adds `.kb-card-dropped` and takes the dim tier: nothing is wrong with it and nothing is waiting on it, so it is not a state colour.
- **Order inside a column** is expedite, then hand-set position, then priority rank, then id — position is the truth and priority is a badge.

### Metrics Strip
- **Shape:** a `flex-wrap: wrap` row of monospace 12px spans with a 14px gap, `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`, `word-break: break-word`, and `role="group" aria-label="Flow metrics"`. The role is not decoration: ARIA 1.2 prohibits a name on a role-less `div`, so the label was inert until the role existed.
- **The two leads:** `WIP n/limit` (the sum of the In Progress and Review limits) and `Oldest Xd vs Yd SLE · N past`, each a `.kb-metric-lead` span (600 weight) wrapping a `<b>` label. They answer *is anything stuck?* without a click, and they are the strip's only permanent content. **The WIP lead names its own limbs in a `title`** — `In Progress 2/2 + Review 0/1` — because `WIP 2/3` sums two numbers the lane headers print separately and can look self-contradictory beside them; the sum is not spelled out on the surface, which the distill removed for a reason.
- **The two disclosures:** `More metrics` opens three `.kb-dim` facts — `Cycle median Nd · 85th percentile Nd · mean Nd`, `Throughput N/7d · N/30d`, and `SLE P% ≤ Nd (from history | from default)`. The three cycle readings are one fact told three ways and share one span, so the reveal stays three items rather than nine; a metric the store cannot compute prints `—`. `Host` beside it opens the revision and the transport's address (`rev N · host … · source`), which is developer vocabulary and lives with the other technical facts rather than on the header's front line. The store's field names never reach the screen: `p85` is the 85th percentile and the SLE's `guess` says `from default`. The full metrics, including each band's own basis, stay in `kanban_metrics`.
- **Emptiness:** the strip prints nothing until the first payload arrives, and `—` stands in for a metric the store cannot compute. It is gated by *content*, not by existence: before the payload it renders its own **empty box** (`.kb-metrics` with the group role and no children), because an absent child is what moved the board — measured, the lane row started 34.00px higher while loading and settled at 0.00px once the box was always there. No fact is invented to fill it: no lead, no disclosure, no `0/0` from a Definition of Workflow this file has not been given. The pending state does not live here: it lives on the board row (see below). Because the strip is payload-gated, so is `Host`: while the board cannot be read there is nothing to name, and the error notice names the URL, the origin and the document instead.

### Inputs / Fields
- **Style:** 1px `border-l1` border, transparent background, `color: inherit`, 7px radius, `padding: 3px 8px`, `height: 26px` (the shared `--kb-control-height`, `box-sizing: border-box`), `font: inherit`, `min-width: 140px`. Types in use: `search` for the card filter, `text` for new-card title, comment and the three reason fields, `number` for position (narrowed to `min-width: 76px`), and `<select>` for priority, class of service and the exception target (all narrowed to `min-width: 0` with a tighter right padding of 4px, because a value picker is not a search field). Every select is inside the drawer: the view row's single-state narrowing was removed with the row, so the toolbar holds two inputs and a button, and no control outside the drawer has a picker.
- **A drawer row's field takes the row's width.** `input.kb-input` and `select.kb-input` are `flex: 1 1 auto` **inside `.kb-drawer .kb-bar` only**. Measured before that rule, the comment field was 140px in a 927px row and every drawer bar's last control stopped 486–756px short of the row's right content edge (88 findings over 6 states) — a form at 15% of its container does not read as narrow-on-purpose, it reads as broken. The page toolbar and the metrics strip keep their spacer-led layout, so the panel's own rows did not change. A select's `padding-right: 4px` keeps its arrow clear of the new edge.
- **Hover:** the border steps up to `border-l2`. **Focus:** the shared 2px brand outline at 2px offset.
- **Error / Disabled:** no error state and no disabled state is styled; validation is expressed by leaving the paired button disabled until the field has content. Placeholders are lowercase and trailing-ellipsised: `search cards…`, `new card…`, `comment…`, `reason to drop…`, `reason to block…`, `reason to except…`. The `::placeholder` rule sets **`opacity: 1`** as well as the colour, because Chrome multiplies whatever an author sets by its own ~0.54: the passing `label-secondary` arrived as 2.27:1 light / 3.35–4.33:1 dark until the rule said so. With the opacity reset, the colour the token gives is the colour that paints, and only the deliberate dark-theme `label-tertiary` rule changes it (8.54:1).

### Notices
- **Shape:** 1px `border-l1`, 7px radius, `padding: 7px 11px`, a **centre-aligned** `flex-wrap: wrap` row. It was baseline-aligned, and on a *two-line* message that put the one-line button against the first line's ink and left the block top-heavy (measured: top spread 6.00px, bottom spread 6.39px). Centre is what the row's own `align-items: center` means and it is stable however many lines the message wraps to. The message's span is `flex: 1 1 auto`, and the strip renders a `.kb-spacer` before its button, so Dismiss sits at the content edge — measured before, it stopped **486.09px** short of it, at 45% of a 968px row, because the sentence did not stretch.
- **Kinds:** `refusal` and `error` recolour the border to `state-error`; `info` recolours it to `brand-primary`. The message is the product's own words; the refusal's machine vocabulary is a monospace `missing: …` span in `label-secondary`; a Dismiss button borrows the button rules.
- **Live:** the strip is `role="status" aria-live="polite"`. It is one of **three** live regions, and the doc used to say two: the strip, the board-level error notice (both of them *conditional states*, present only while they have something to say), and the persistent announcement span the board writes every state change into — which must be mounted on every pass, because a live region inserted together with its first message is a region many screen readers never announce. No message is written into two of them, so nothing is spoken twice. `role="status"` on the first two is not a fourth mechanism: it implies `aria-live="polite"` on the element it names.
- **The announcer is visually hidden, and its message does not persist.** That span carries `.kb-vh` — the sheet's one utility class, a 1px clipped box (`position: absolute`, `overflow: hidden`, `clip-path: inset(50%)`) that is out of the page's flex layout and out of the 10px gap while staying in the accessibility tree. It is deliberately not `display: none` or `visibility: hidden`, which would remove it from that tree and silence the region. Its text is also cleared five seconds after it is written: the region is a thing said once, not state the panel keeps, and a stray `moving T-004 to review` line used to outlive the move it described. This paragraph is the correction of an earlier claim that the span "paints nothing" while it was in fact a visible `.kb-dim` child of the page.

### Signature Component: the Two-Number Metrics Strip

The strip is the distill pass's clearest statement: **the two numbers that answer the urgent question are permanent, and the history that answers a slower one costs one click.** WIP against its limit and the oldest card against the SLE fit on one 12px line; cycle, throughput and the SLE's own basis do not deserve that line's width until someone asks. The order inside the row is fixed — two leads, then the two disclosures, then the revealed facts — so the strip has one scan order instead of nine equal items, and each disclosure keeps its position whether it is open or closed.

### Signature Component: the Card's Dual Path

A card is not a list row. It is a `role="button"` that carries three independent interactions on one surface: pointer drag with a drop index computed from the pointer's Y against each card's midpoint, `Alt+←/→` to move it one lane along the board order (announcing "T-006 moved to review", or "T-006 is already at the end of the board" at either end — a dropped card included, because `dropped` is the last lane of that order: `Alt+←` walks a dropped card back to `Done`), and `Enter` (or `Space`, the other half of the button pattern) or a click to open the drawer. The midpoints are measured **once per drag**, not once per `dragover`: the cards do not move while a drag is live, so re-reading every rect on every event was layout work whose answer could not have changed. All three land in the same store call, so the keyboard path passes the same gates as the drag — a refusal reads the same either way. Cards are sorted with `expedite` first so the one exception to WIP control is visible without opening anything.

### Signature Component: the Checklist Row

The Definition of Ready and Definition of Done rows are `kb-check kb-btn` — a button that borrows the whole button vocabulary (radius, hairline, hover, press nudge, focus ring, disabled) and adds a drawn glyph at 14px, `viewBox="0 0 16 16"`, one 1.5 stroke in `currentColor`, `aria-hidden`, with the tick path rendered only when the item is checked. `aria-pressed` is the only source of truth for the state, and the unticked case appends the words `· unticked`, because the amber that colours it in dark cannot carry small text on white. Every row is disabled while the store's `busy` is set — a tick that cannot land is not offered — so a ticked row is briefly both chosen and disabled, which is the one case the sheet's `[aria-pressed="true"]:disabled` rule exists for.

### Signature Component: the Drawer

The drawer is the last child of the page, inline and scrollable, never a modal and never an overlay: it is `role="region"` with `aria-label="{id} {title}"` — named, because a role-less `div` may not carry a label and an unnamed region is a landmark a reader cannot use — it takes focus when it opens (`tabIndex: -1`), and it records *why* it was opened — `data-open="keyboard"` when `Enter` on a card moved the keyboard there, `data-open="pointer"` otherwise — so the programmatic focus paints its ring on the keyboard path without giving the pointer path a ring it did not earn, then hands focus back to the card it came from when it closes, `Escape` is a convenience beside the Close button rather than the only way out, and `Tab` leaves it like any other box. Its header is one wrapping row of chips (id, title, state, priority, class, age) and the keyboard hint `Alt+←/→ moves · Esc closes`. Its body leads with **one primary move** — the next state in the board's own order — beside a `More moves…` disclosure; behind that fold sit every other target, the numeric position with its `Set position` button, `Exception to a gate…` and its target/reason pair, and the `block` and `drop` groups with their reason fields, the destructive group set off by a full-width hairline and 7px of padding. The two checklists and the comment stay visible: they are the gate and the conversation. Its history rows print the event's kind and the **actor's kind**, not the raw `agent:<session>` token, which is kept in the row's `title`. `Unblock` is the exception — it sits on the blocked notice that names the blocker, because a blocked card is the one case where the obvious move is not a move. A successful **Drop** is acknowledged through the notice strip (`T-006 dropped — still on the board, under Dropped`), because the card leaving the board used to be silent and its way back was a view nobody would guess; the lane it names is now on screen by default, so the sentence is a pointer rather than the only route.

### Signature Component: the Sidebar Icon

An inline SVG, `viewBox="0 0 20 20"`, `fill: none`, `stroke: currentColor`, `aria-hidden`, `strokeWidth` 1.4 at rest and 1.8 when the row is active. Three rounded bars of decreasing height (`rx: 1.2`) at x = 2.5 / 8.1 / 13.7, y = 3, heights 14 / 10 / 7 — a board seen down its lanes. It takes `{ size, active }` from the sidebar row and defaults to 20px when `size` is not a number. The entry is labelled **Kanban**.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** style every colour through a `--dsw-*` token with a literal fallback, exactly as `styles.ts` does — the app's theme is the palette, and it must work in both modes without an edit.
- **Do** use `border-l1` hairlines, the radius scale (7/8/10/999px) and the three background tiers to separate surfaces; that is the whole depth vocabulary.
- **Do** give every interactive control the shared 2px brand focus ring (2px offset; 1px on a card), and never leave a control on the browser default.
- **Do** signal a card's exception by recolouring its whole 1px border in the state colour — `--dsw-alias-state-error-primary` for expedite, `--dsw-alias-state-warn-primary` for blocked — and let the chip text name the state.
- **Do** de-emphasise with `--dsw-alias-label-secondary`, and use `--dsw-alias-label-tertiary` only as a dark-theme enhancement inside `body[data-ds-dark-theme]`.
- **Do** keep a colour that fails AA on light out of a text rule unless it is scoped to the dark marker; the panel's own test enforces exactly this.
- **Do** reveal progressive detail with the disclosure primitive: a real `<button type="button">` carrying `aria-expanded`, a stable label, and the revealed half rendered inline in the row it belongs to.
- **Do** keep the two metrics numbers permanent and put the rest of the history behind `More metrics`; a one-click cost is the right price for a second question.
- **Do** lead the drawer with one primary move, and keep the checklists and the comment visible while everything rarer hides behind `More moves…`.
- **Do** keep lanes at `flex: 0 0 236px` inside an `overflow-x: auto` row with a 140px floor, and narrow an empty lane to a content-width dashed slot whose name and count stay on one horizontal line — opening to full width while a drag is live.
- **Do** keep the card drawer inline, below the board, capped at `max-height: 260px` with its own scroll.
- **Do** make a pressed control a tint (`bg-layer-2`) plus `border-l2` plus weight 600 — never a colour on its own.
- **Do** keep the 140ms ease-out transitions as the only motion, and cancel them under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.
- **Do** use monospace for card ids, lane counts, priority codes, flow metrics, history rows and the header's count line, and draw every icon as inline SVG in one stroke width with `currentColor`.
- **Do** keep only what demands a decision on the card — `blocked`, the P0/P1 word, a non-`fresh` age band, `expedite` — and let the drawer print the card's filing in full.
- **Do** keep a keyboard path for every pointer action — `Enter` **and** `Space` on a card, `Alt+←/→` to move it — and announce state changes through the persistent `aria-live` region.

### Don't:
- **Don't** introduce a colour that is not one of the app's `--dsw-*` tokens. Invented `--dsw-*`/`--dsh-*` names resolve to nothing and fall back silently; the panel shipped ten of them once and was off-theme in both modes.
- **Don't** hardcode a hex, `rgba()` or `oklch()` value as a design colour — the literals in `styles.ts` are fallbacks for absent tokens.
- **Don't** add `box-shadow`, a gradient, a glass/blur treatment, or a `border-left`/`border-right` (or any one-sided border) above 1px. The 3px coloured side stripe the panel once used is banned by the craft floor and has been removed.
- **Don't** assume `--dsw-alias-brand-primary` is a hue. It resolves to the app's foreground value; a brand-coloured fill or a brand-coloured pressed state is the bug that shipped once already.
- **Don't** carry text in amber or in `--dsw-alias-label-tertiary` outside `body[data-ds-dark-theme]`.
- **Don't** use `opacity` as the de-emphasis ladder — the label tokens are the ladder. **Three instances of element opacity were found by three independent reviews and all three are gone**: `0.5` on a disabled button (its label measured 3.51:1), `0.6` on the busy board row (2.53:1 light), and the UA's own ~0.54 on every placeholder (2.27:1 light). The sheet now declares exactly **one** opacity — `opacity: 1` in the `::placeholder` rule, which *restores* what the token says rather than dimming it — and a test walks the sheet and fails on any selector that dims a box.
- **Don't** put a name on a role-less `div`. ARIA 1.2's generic role prohibits it, which made four `aria-label`s inert (the metrics strip, both checklist groups, the lane): each now carries the role it actually is (`group`, `region`) before it carries a name.
- **Don't** leave an `aria-keyshortcuts` on an element that cannot be focused: the lane declared the card's shortcut for two passes and no reader could act on it. The card, which is focusable and does offer the shortcut, keeps it.
- **Don't** paint a page background — `.kb-page`, notices, the drawer, buttons, inputs and cards stay transparent-by-token so the host panel shows through.
- **Don't** restore a view row, a state filter or a lane that is drawn on demand: the board draws every state and the last lane is `dropped`, which is what keeps a dropped card reachable. Filters narrow cards, never lanes.
- **Don't** use red, amber or green for emphasis, size or decoration.
- **Don't** make the drawer, the drop-reason prompt or any board action a modal dialog — dropping needs a written reason, so it is an inline field whose button stays disabled until there is one.
- **Don't** animate a disclosure open; the revealed half appears in the same frame, separated by a hairline rather than by motion.
- **Don't** build a disclosure out of `<details>` or re-implement it with local state and a renamed label — `aria-expanded` on a real button is the contract.
- **Don't** use monospace for prose, titles or button labels.
- **Don't** let a state be carried by colour alone: the card prints the word, the lane prints `M/N`, the unticked row prints `· unticked`.

18. **Toggles and disclosures share one visual vocabulary — (resolved in this pass).** They share the *treatment* on purpose (see 17) and are told apart by two things that are not colour: the caret, which only a disclosure has, and the attribute itself — `aria-pressed` on a toggle, `aria-expanded` on a disclosure. A reader who cannot see the tint still gets a different shape and a different state name.
19. **The disclosure toggle is the smallest hit target on the panel — (resolved in the polish pass; the height re-stated by the align fix pass).** Every `.kb-btn` carries a control height — `min-height: 24px` in the polish pass, and the align fix pass replaced that floor with the **26px `--kb-control-height`** shared by the button, the input and the select, because a floor never capped the 30.39px natural box and a bar holding a button and an input measured two heights. `.kb-more` is an inline-flex box inside the same 26px, so the smallest control by *type* is no longer the smallest by *target*. The visual weight is unchanged: the padding, the font size and the transparent ground are exactly as they were, apart from the 9px→8px inset that gives one bar one left edge.
20. **The parked lane hides its own empty-state text — (resolved in this pass: kept, and bounded; the doc's promise of it corrected by the validation pass, and re-stated here after the rail became a slot).** A content-width `.kb-lane-empty` slot still renders the name on one horizontal line and the count beside it, and `nothing here` stays hidden at rest (`.kb-lane-empty .kb-empty { display: none }`) — the slot exists to be narrow, and that text is what would make it 236px; the moment a drag is live the lane is awake (`.kb-lane-awake`, back to 236px) and the line returns (`display: block`). The information stays available to assistive technology through the lane's `aria-label` (`"Review, 0 cards, limit 1"`, plus `", over the limit"` past the cap), and the slot no longer lies during a first load: a pending board draws a skeleton line in each lane and is `aria-busy` at the row, so `nothing here` is only said when it is the answer. **One other place in this document still promised the line as always-on** — the slot's summary in *Key Characteristics* — and the validation pass corrected it rather than restoring text to a resting lane, because the code was right and the prose was stale. The geometry that replaced the rail is recorded in the slot paragraph above: no `writing-mode` at all, a dashed decorative edge, and width rather than tint as the thing that distinguishes the parked lane.

21. **The dark-theme resolutions are documented against the app's `bg-base`, and the card sits on a lighter surface.** `label-tertiary` is 8.54:1 against `#151517` but 7.36:1 against the lane/card tier `#232324`, red is 5.55:1 against the base but 4.77:1 against the lane, and amber is 8.49:1 against the base but 7.31:1 against the lane. All still pass, so no rule changes — the *surface* each colour actually sits on is now recorded per row in **Measured contrast**, and this entry stands as the record of what the first draft measured against instead. This regeneration still did not measure against the host's actual sidebar fill (which the panel never paints over and therefore cannot itself guarantee).
22. **The scene for light-versus-dark is not recorded.** The craft floor asks the theme to be picked from the use scene rather than by category. This panel does not choose at all: it follows the app's active theme. PRODUCT.md states that as a commitment, so the light/dark question belongs to the app and not to this system — recorded here because a design system that inherits its theme should say so rather than appear undecided.
23. **(new, this pass) The scrollbar thumb cannot reach 3:1 from the app's own tokens.** Measured on the surface it actually sits on, the thumb is 1.26:1 in light (neutral-200 on the white lane) and 1.43:1 in dark (neutral-700 on `#232324`); its hover step reaches 1.48:1 / 2.10:1. The panel's rule forbids an invented colour and the ladder has no darker rung the app points at a thumb, so the shortfall is recorded rather than papered over with a literal. What would settle it is an app token for a scrollbar thumb with 3:1 against `bg-layer-1` — a request for the app's palette, not a change this panel should make on its own. The 1.00:1 defect this pass *did* fix was different in kind: it was a track painted the same value as the thumb, which is why the track is now the app's own `transparent`.
24. **(new, this pass) The card's hairline is its only boundary in light.** `bg-base` and `bg-layer-1` both resolve to `#fff` in light, and `border-l1` (`#0000000a`, compositing to `#f5f5f5`) over that is 1.09:1 — so a resting card is separated from its lane by a 1px edge that measures as nothing. WCAG exempts a purely decorative boundary, and the opening listed `border-l2` (1.26:1, `#0000001a`) as a possible fix; it was declined because the system's own rule gives `border-l2` two meaning-bearing consumers (a pressed button, a hovered input) and a resting card is not a state. The defect stands recorded, and it is the reason the lane's own edge and the radius step carry as much of the card's shape as they do.

## Known Defects

- **A forced read that lands mid-write can still replace the optimistic payload — the timer's poll no longer can.** The fix gives `refresh` no ownership of the write it is not part of: `busy` belongs to the `write` that set it and only `write` clears it, and a read that is not forced returns without applying its answer once it *lands* while `busy` is set (checked after the await, because the race is the answer arriving mid-write, not the timer firing mid-write). So the timer can neither switch the `kb-lanes-busy` treatment off early nor overwrite the just-moved card. Both halves are pinned by the store-level case in `test/client.dom.test.ts` that fails on the pre-fix code. **What remains:** a forced read — the header's `Refresh` or the error notice's `Retry`, pressed while a write is in flight — is still applied, and still overwrites the optimistic payload with the board as of that read. That is a deliberate human action rather than the timer's, and it takes a concurrent *external* change to the same card for the snap-back to be visible, so it stays a **risk, not a proven bug**. The guard attempted in the polish pass (a write sequence number so a refresh would only end its own write) did not hold under test and was reverted rather than shipped half-working; the attempt left **no trace in git**, so the shape of the failure is recorded here rather than in a diff.

- **The align fix pass's measured before/after (probe of 2026-10-17, 84 states: 14 states × 2 themes × 3 widths).** Findings fell from **1,918 to 850**, and the four metric families read: `lane-width` **66 → 0**, `control-height` **210 → 0**, `trailing-slack` **264 → 132**, `spread-explained` **1,378 → 718**. The panel's computed `font-size` is **13px in all 84 states** (`bodySize: 16px`); the lane carrying a long unbreakable token measures **236.00px** against its declared basis, where it measured **603.03px**; the drawer rows that were 486–756px short of the content edge measure **0** (the comment row and the three reason/select rows each leave the finding list); the page notice's Dismiss measures **0** (from **486.09px**, and the notice leaves the report entirely); `escape-panel`, `escape-row`, `overlap`, `row-edge`, `gap-mismatch`, `gap-vs-declared` and `leading-slack` are **0** before and after; the loading-versus-loaded lane-row top shift is **34.00px → 0.00px**. The pre-fix figures are that same probe run against the previous commit, with its report kept aside before the after-run. Every changed test was run against the pre-fix bundle: **14** of the align cases failed there, and the two that passed (`A12`, `A17`) record facts that were already correct.

- **The trailing slack that remains is drawn, not measured away.** 132 findings survive, and every one of them is a *button* at the end of a bar that has nothing left to grow into: the drawer's action bars — `Exception to a gate…` (measured, one row's button stops **782.38px** short of its 927px row), `More moves…` (**738.58px**) and a card's primary move button, `→ Dropped` (**599.41px**) — and the metrics strip's `Host` disclosure (**481.05px** short of the 968px strip). The brief's item 3 named the *form* rows and item 4 the notice, and both now measure 0: what changed is that a field grows and pushes its button to the edge, while a lone action button has no content to stretch to. The two ways to remove the number — `justify-content: flex-end` on the bar, or stretching the button across the row — would each move a control away from the label it belongs to or make a one-word button 900px wide, so both were declined and the number is recorded here instead. This is the honest remainder of the panel's "floating button" pattern.
